🌳 Five-Senses Grounding

Gently notice the world around you. There is no need to rush.

  1. Name five things you can see.
  2. Notice four things you can feel.
  3. Listen for three sounds.
  4. Identify two smells, if comfortable.
  5. Notice one taste or take a comfortable breath.

Other Healthy Ways to Unwind

  • Take a gentle walk or stretch in a way that feels comfortable.
  • Listen to peaceful music or an encouraging SDA hymn.
  • Spend time outdoors and notice your surroundings.
  • Speak to a trusted person or write in a journal.
  • Take a break, drink water or follow a regular sleep routine.

Overcoming Drug and Substance Abuse

Substance use problems can affect health, relationships, learning and everyday life. They are treatable, and seeking support is a positive step.

Possible Effects

  • Problems with memory, concentration and decision-making.
  • Changes in mood, sleep and behaviour.
  • Physical health complications that vary by substance.
  • Conflict in relationships and difficulties at school or work.
  • Accidents, overdose and other serious risks.

Steps Toward Recovery

  1. Recognize the impact of substance use on your life.
  2. Speak with a qualified healthcare or addiction professional.
  3. Identify triggers and plan healthier ways to respond.
  4. Build supportive relationships and regular routines.
  5. Follow professional treatment recommendations.
  6. Seek support again if difficulties return.
Dependence on alcohol or sedatives can cause dangerous withdrawal. Seek medical guidance before stopping suddenly. If someone collapses, cannot be awakened or has difficulty breathing, seek emergency medical assistance immediately.

Child Discipline and Positive Parenting

Positive discipline teaches responsibility and self-control while respecting a child's dignity and emotional wellbeing.

Practical Principles

  1. Set clear, realistic, age-appropriate expectations.
  2. Explain rules and listen to children's questions.
  3. Recognize effort and positive behaviour.
  4. Use consistent, proportionate, non-violent consequences.
  5. Model patience, respect and accountability.
  6. Help children name emotions and solve problems.
  7. Offer choices within reasonable boundaries.
  8. Keep communication open as children grow.
  9. Encourage independence appropriate to their age.
  10. Repair relationships after disagreements.

What to Avoid

  • Physical punishment, threats and humiliation.
  • Insults or hurtful comparisons between children.
  • Unpredictable rules and disproportionate consequences.
Example: If a child refuses to study, calmly explore what is making the task difficult. Break it into smaller steps, agree on a routine and offer encouragement as the child progresses.

Mental Health and Emotional Wellbeing

Mental health affects how we think, feel, relate to others and manage life's challenges. Everyone deserves care, respect and support.

Ways to Support Your Wellbeing

  • Maintain a regular sleep routine.
  • Engage in physical activity suited to your abilities.
  • Eat balanced meals and drink enough water.
  • Talk with trusted people about your feelings.
  • Use grounding, comfortable breathing or quiet reflection.
  • Set realistic goals and healthy boundaries.
  • Seek professional support when difficulties persist.

Possible Warning Signs

  • Persistent sadness, anxiety or hopelessness.
  • Withdrawing from people or activities you enjoy.
  • Major changes in sleep or appetite.
  • Difficulty concentrating or managing daily responsibilities.
  • Feeling overwhelmed for a prolonged period.

These signs do not automatically mean someone has a mental illness. A qualified professional can help assess persistent or worsening symptoms.

Depression Support

Depression can affect energy, sleep, appetite, concentration and the ability to enjoy everyday activities. It is a health condition, not a personal failure. Professional assessment and appropriate treatment can help.

  • Talk with someone you trust.
  • Break daily tasks into manageable steps.
  • Maintain regular meals and sleep as far as possible.
  • Seek help from a qualified mental health professional.
  • Seek urgent medical assistance if you or another person is in immediate danger.
